# Accessibility Review — ink-vibe

*Lens: Accessibility, within the bmad-ux Reviewer Gate. Reviewed file: `theme-fidelity-spec.md` (2026-07-19 draft) against `EXPERIENCE.md` and `DESIGN.md`. No files were changed.*

## Overall verdict

**Changes requested (conditional pass).** Judged against the project's stated bar — the **NFR-5 readability-first floor** (`EXPERIENCE.md → Accessibility Floor`), *not* a formal WCAG 2.2 AA conformance claim for v1 — the spec gets the single most important thing right: it consistently enforces the DESIGN.md rule that Gradering/winner rank pairs **colour + text + icon** and is never colour-only (§5 AC, §3 badge, Winner banner). That is the headline a11y commitment and it holds.

However, the floor itself explicitly enumerates *"keyboard-operable controls; **visible focus states**; **semantic heading order**; **alt text** on featured images and borg logos; **sufficient contrast**"* — and the spec is silent or incomplete on **focus states, heading-level assignment, alt text beyond borg logos, decorative-element hiding, and reduced-motion coverage**. Those are floor gaps, not merely WCAG nice-to-haves, so they are graded high/medium below.

Separately, per the task, I estimated the actual colour pairs the spec composes and **flag WCAG contrast failures as information** (the project does not claim AA). Most warm-palette pairs land in the 3.4–4.5:1 band — passable for large text / UI but under the 4.5:1 normal-text line. **One pair — gold eyebrow on the gold-tinted winner card (~1.8:1) — is low enough to threaten actual legibility, which makes it a readability-floor breach, not just a WCAG note.**

Severity counts: **critical 1 · high 4 · medium 6 · low 3** (14 findings; includes 1 positive confirmation).

## Findings

### Rank encoding (colour-alone)

- **[low / pass]** Colour-alone rank encoding is correctly prevented. §5 AC requires the winner card's per-rank label to be "paired with **text + icon** (never colour-only)", the §3 type badge pairs the Sparkles icon with the "Uitdaging" word, and the Winner-banner AC orders `algehele wenner` vs `wenner` by a **text** distinction plus prominence — not hue. This honours DESIGN.md's emphatic Gradering a11y rule (§3, §5). *Fix:* none needed; keep this wording verbatim when it lands in Epic 19 stories so it is not lost in implementation.
- **[low]** DESIGN.md warns that Meester's red-orange (`#EA4015`, = the primary action colour) "must always be visibly a Gradering label (text + icon), never a bare swatch that could be read as a button." The winner card (§5) describes only the *gold* rank family and does not carry this Meester-specific caution, and §5 hardcodes a **gold gradient** card even though DESIGN.md's Winner banner says each rank "carries the relevant Brons/Silwer/Goud colour token." A featured Brons/Silwer winner rendered in gold, or a Meester swatch read as a CTA, would erode the text+icon guarantee. *Fix:* add an AC that the winner card's rank tint follows the winner's actual Gradering token (not always gold), and restate the Meester "never a bare swatch" caution.

### Contrast on load-bearing combos (WCAG flagged as info; one elevated on readability grounds)

- **[critical]** **Gold eyebrow on the gold-tinted winner card ≈ 1.8:1** (§5). The eyebrow uses gold text (spec's `#e8b130`, or token `goud #C9B88A` ≈ L 0.485) over a `gold/10 → gold-muted/20` gradient that resolves to a near-surface pale ground — a gold-on-near-white pairing that lands around **1.8:1**, far under even the 3:1 large-text line. This is not merely a WCAG info item: the readability-first floor's own words are "sufficient contrast," and gold body/eyebrow text this faint is genuinely hard to read. *Fix:* do not colour the eyebrow (and any gold-toned label text) in gold on a light ground; use `text`/`muted-text` for the eyebrow words and let the Crown icon + gold *tint* carry the "gold moment," or darken the gold used for text well below the fill gold. The rank word must stay legible.
- **[high]** **Pale badge pill — terracotta text on `primary/10` ≈ 3.6:1** (§2 hero badge 14px/500; §3 card type-badge 12px/600 UPPERCASE). Terracotta (#EA4015, L 0.212) on a 10% terracotta tint over surface resolves to roughly **3.6:1** — below 4.5:1 for normal text, and the §3 badge is 12px (small). WCAG-info under the floor, but load-bearing UI. *Fix:* deepen the pill text toward a darker terracotta/`text` for the small UPPERCASE badge, or raise the tint contrast; alternatively accept as info and record it explicitly as a knowing floor exception.
- **[medium]** **White text on terracotta buttons ≈ 4.0:1** (§0.1, §9). White on `primary` computes to ~4.0:1 — under 4.5 for the `sm`/`default`/`lg` label sizes (14–16px, weight 500, i.e. not "large"). The `primary-light` **hover** fill drops further to ~3.1:1. WCAG-info. *Fix:* acceptable as a documented floor exception for button labels (UI text), but note that hover reduces contrast; consider not lightening the fill as much on hover, or bolding labels to reach the large-text 3:1 line cleanly.
- **[medium]** **`muted-text` #6B7280 body/captions — borderline on surface (~4.48:1), failing on cream (~4.0:1)** (§2 body, §4 labels, §6 read-time/counts/excerpt, §10 footer). Muted text passes on `surface-alt` (~4.7:1) but is borderline on `surface` and **fails 4.5:1 on the cream bands** the spec places it on: `secondary/30` (§5), `secondary/20` (§7), footer `secondary/30` (§10). Captions at 12px are small text. WCAG-info. *Fix:* on cream-tinted sections use `text` (not `muted-text`) for body/caption, or nudge the muted token darker; at minimum record which surfaces knowingly sit under 4.5:1.
- **[medium]** **White/80 body on the terracotta CTA gradient ≈ 3.4:1** (§8; body is 18px regular = not "large" per WCAG). Pure white on that band is only ~4.0:1 and the 80%-opacity body drops to ~3.4:1. Also in §8 the CTA primary button is **terracotta text on white ≈ 3.7:1**. WCAG-info. *Fix:* use full-opacity white for the CTA body rather than white/80, and treat the terracotta-on-white button label as a documented UI exception.
- **[medium]** **Sage eyebrow / sage ghost-button text ≈ 4.2:1 on surface, ~4.0:1 on cream** (§1 "My profiel" ghost sage hover, §7 sage eyebrow 14px/500). Sage `#4D8066` on the warm grounds lands just under 4.5:1 for normal text. WCAG-info. *Fix:* darken sage for text use, or accept as floor exception; the sage *fill* button (white on sage) is fine — this is only sage-as-text.

### Focus states

- **[high]** **Keyboard focus is unspecified for every WP target.** The floor explicitly requires "visible focus states," and the spec notes the Lovable button uses `focus-visible:ring-2` — but that requirement is **not carried into any WP acceptance criterion**: §0.1/§9 (buttons), §1 (nav links, header CTAs), §3/§5/§6 (cards-as-links, "Sien alle werke"), and form/CTA controls all describe hover but never focus. Hover-only styling leaves keyboard users with no visible focus indicator. *Fix:* add a global AC (best in §0) requiring a visible `:focus-visible` ring/outline (e.g. 2px terracotta or sage offset ring) on all buttons, nav links, card links, and interactive controls, mirroring the Lovable `focus-visible:ring-2`; ensure the ring itself meets 3:1 against adjacent colour.

### Reduced motion

- **[high]** **`prefers-reduced-motion` coverage is partial and mis-scoped.** The only reduced-motion clause (§0.6) sits inside the "Mount animations (optional, low priority) … *Nice-to-have; not fidelity-blocking*" section and says "disable transforms" — but the motion the spec actually mandates elsewhere is **not** covered there: the **card hover-lift `translateY(-4px)`** (§0.5, and applied in §3/§6), the **sponsor `hover:scale-105`** (§7), the **CTA/button transitions** (§0.1), and the underline-slide. Because those live in non-optional sections with no reduced-motion note, a reader could ship hover-lift and hover-scale while treating reduced-motion as an optional afterthought. *Fix:* promote a single normative AC covering **all** motion — fade-up, underline-slide, hover-lift transform, hover:scale, and transitions — under `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)`, and remove the "optional/nice-to-have" framing from the reduced-motion obligation (the animations may be optional; honouring the query is not).

### Texture legibility

- **[low]** **Plus-pattern texture is safe as specced, but not marked decorative.** §0.4 puts the plus-pattern at opacity **0.03**, behind content (`z-index` below), non-click-intercepting — at 3% over surface this does not threaten hero-text legibility. Good. The only gap: it is a purely decorative layer and the spec does not state it must be `aria-hidden`/CSS-generated (a `::before`/background is already invisible to AT, which is fine). *Fix:* confirm it is implemented as a CSS background/`::before` (not an `<img>`); if ever an inline element, mark `aria-hidden`.

### Semantic structure & alt text

- **[medium]** **Heading levels are not systematically assigned; skip/duplication risk.** Only §2 (hero `h1`) and §3 (challenge card `h2`) state explicit levels. Section titles in §5, §6, §8 and card titles in §5/§6 are described only by font size (e.g. "title Lora 24→30px/600"), leaving the level ambiguous. The §8 CTA heading is the same 30→48px size as the hero `h1` and could be coded as a second `h1`; card titles could skip from a section `h2` straight past `h3`. *Fix:* add a heading-outline AC — one `h1` (hero), each major section title `h2` (§5/§6/§7/§8), card titles `h3`, sub-labels non-heading — and state that heading level is independent of the visual size token.
- **[high]** **Alt text is required only for borg logos; avatars and images are uncovered.** The floor calls for "alt text on **featured images** and borg logos," but only §7 carries an alt-text AC. The **author/winner avatars** (§5 48×48, §6 32×32) and any featured/card imagery have no alt-text or accessible-name requirement. *Fix:* add an AC that every avatar has meaningful alt (author name) or is marked decorative with the name exposed as adjacent text, and that any featured image carries alt text (or is decorative + `aria-hidden` when purely illustrative).
- **[medium]** **Decorative shapes/watermarks/icons are not marked `aria-hidden`.** The decorative corner tints (§3, §5 `rounded-bl` quarter-circles), the Crown **watermark** (§5), the two faint CTA circles (§8), and the many in-button/in-badge icons (BookOpen, Sparkles, Trophy, Crown, PenLine, arrow) are all decorative but the spec never requires them to be hidden from AT. *Fix:* add an AC that decorative shapes/watermarks and icons that duplicate adjacent text are `aria-hidden="true"`.
- **[medium]** **Reaction-count icons carry meaning without a text label.** §6 shows "Heart 16px + count / MessageCircle 16px + count" — a bare number beside an icon, with no word. Per EXPERIENCE.md voice the count is verb-less *because the icon does the work*, which means the icon is **meaningful** and must expose an accessible name; a screen reader otherwise hears only "342". *Fix:* give the Heart/MessageCircle counts an accessible label (e.g. `aria-label` "342 hartjies", `_n()`-pluralised for `af`), or render a visually-hidden "hartjies"/"reaksies" word next to the count.

### Target size & hit areas

- **[medium]** **Primary CTAs at 36px sit under the comfortable 44px touch target on mobile.** Buttons scale sm 36 / default 40 / lg 44 / xl 48 (§9); the **header "Begin skryf" and "My profiel" are `sm` 36px** (§1) and the §3 challenge-card CTA is 40px. The hero's `lg` 44px buttons are fine. 36–40px clears the WCAG 2.5.8 (2.2 AA) **24px** minimum, so this is not a WCAG-AA fail, but it is under the 44px comfortable/AAA target for a primary mobile action. *Fix:* on mobile, promote the primary submission CTA and the in-card CTA to ≥44px height (or ≥44px tap area via padding), even if the desktop visual stays 36–40px.
- **[low]** **Footer and nav links are small, closely stacked tap targets.** Footer link columns use 14px text with `space-y-2` (8px gaps) (§10) and nav links are 16px in a 32px-gap row (§1); the effective tap height may fall below 44px and links sit close together. Clears the 24px WCAG-AA minimum. *Fix:* ensure link tap areas are ≥44px tall via vertical padding on mobile, keeping the 24px-min spacing comfortably exceeded.
